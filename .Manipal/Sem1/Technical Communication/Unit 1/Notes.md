# Introduction to Communication
## Meaning
- Communication, in simple terms, is the transfer of information between people, resulting in mutual understanding. Communication has been defined differently by various writers and
behavioural theorists. 
- communication is an exchange of facts, ideas, opinions, or emotions.
- Communication involves words, symbols, messages, and is a way to share meaning between individuals.

## Popular Defenition
• According to Newman and Summer, "Communication is an exchange of facts, ideas,
opinions, or emotions by two or more persons."
• According to Allen Louis, "It is the sum of all the things one person does when they want
to create an understanding in the mind of another."
• Bellows, Gilson, and Odirone define communication as "A communion by words, letters,
symbols, or messages, and as a way that one organisation member shares meaning with
the other."
• Hoben defines communication as "The verbal interchange of thoughts or ideas."
• In the words of Anderson, "Communication is the process by which we understand
others and, in turn, endeavour to be understood by them. It is dynamic, constantly
changing and shifting in response to the total situation."
• According to Berelson and Steiner, "Communication is the transmission of information,
ideas, emotions, skills, etc. by the use of symbols, words, pictures, figures, graphs, etc."
• In the words of Fotheringham, "Communication is a process involving the selection,
production, and transmission of signs in such a way as to help a receiver perceive a
meaning similar to that in the mind of the communicator."

## Characteristics of Communication
- Inescapable:
    Communication is an inevitable aspect of human interaction; even
    without using words, we unintentionally communicate all the time. Our body language,
    attire, punctuality, behavior, and the physical environment we occupy are all signifiers
that convey messages to others.
- Two-way Exchange of Information:
    Communication involves the sharing of information
    between two or more individuals, with simultaneous feedback.
- Process Oriented:
    Each message is part of a broader process and does not operate in
    isolation. The meaning attached to a message depends on preceding events and the
    present context. For instance, a boss's response to a promotion request is influenced
    by the past relationship with the employee and the boss's current mood.
- Sender-receiver dynamic:
    Communication commences with a sender transmitting a
    message, necessitating a receiver to interpret and assign meaning to that message.
- Verbal and Non-verbal nature:
    Communication encompasses verbal and non-verbal
    forms. It employs spoken or written words, as well as body language, such as gestures
    and facial expressions.
- Successful Interpretation:
    Communication succeeds when the receiver interprets the
    intended meaning in alignment with the sender's intention. Misinterpretation of a
    message signifies communication failure, which can arise from various factors explored
    later in this module.
- Dynamic Process:
    Communication entails the meaningful exchange of thoughts and
    experiences while assimilating, processing, and responding to the conversation
    partner. The mode and manner of communication may vary depending on the
    circumstances.
- Facilitates Understanding:
    Communication enables individuals to comprehend the
    necessity for change, the rationale behind certain actions, the process of implementing
    processes, and the importance of their own contributions.

## Types of Communication
There are Two Types
- Verbal communication:
- Non-Verbal Communication
### Verbal Communication 
- refers to communication that uses spoken
or unspoken words, as in the case of symbolic language (signs). It is important to grasp how
to effectively communicate your ideas verbally to avoid misunderstandings and amass
interest in your speech. Using the right type of language, speaking clearly, knowing your
audience, and responding in an appropriate manner are all essential to mastering the art of
verbal communication

- Oral communication refers to the verbal transmission of messages from one individual or
group to another. In business settings, oral communication includes meetings, presentations,
one-on-one discussions, performance reviews, etc. For example, a manager providing
feedback during a performance review or a sales manager explaining new sales targets in a
team meeting

- Written communication involves correspondence through the written word. It can be
handwritten, printed, or typed. An example of Written Communication is a manager
composing an apology letter responding to a customer complaint about poor service.
Workplace written communication includes letters, memos, circulars, notices, reports, and
emails.

#### Types of Verbal Communication
There are two Categories Written and Oral. The below ones are the types of Verbal communication

- Intrapersonal Communication - Your private verbal communication channel. You talk to
yourself (self-talk) and express your thoughts. Communicating with yourself will give you
more confidence and clarity in your thinking. It will help you make decisions, form sentences,
choose suitable words, and find effective ways to connect with others. This will help you gain
your audience's trust.

- Interpersonal Communication - Also known as one-to-one verbal communication between
two people. It helps you grasp if you're conveying your thoughts clearly. Reactions,
responses, and verbal/nonverbal cues from the other person show if you're being
understood. Communication is more than just talking to someone; it is also about listening.
So listen, think, then respond. Take time to think and avoid offending people with your
response.

- Small Group Communication - The number of participants increases. You go from
communicating with one person to a few more. These small groups could be team meetings,
board meetings, or sales meetings. The number of participants is small enough for everyone
to communicate together. When attending small group meetings, be prepared with a topic to
stay on track. Thoughts.

- Public Communication - Also known as public speaking. Here, an individual addresses a large
audience simultaneously. Speeches, campaigns, and presentations are examples of public
communication. Since the audience size is more extensive, use words and phrases they'll
quickly grasp and structure your thoughts before addressing them. The more prepared you
are, the more confident you'll feel as a public speaker.

#### Advantages of Verbal Communication
Verbal communication is one of the most important mediums of communication. The
stronger your communication skills are, the easier it is for you to establish trust and build
lasting relationships with others. Here are some benefits of strong verbal communication
skills:
- It provides complete understanding and there's room to clarify any messages that may
have been misunderstood.
- It's one of the fastest modes of communication and is time-efficient.
- There is space for providing feedback, which allows two or more people to engage in a
conversation at the same time.
- It allows speakers to exercise influence and persuade listeners to agree with ideas,
thoughts, and opinions.
- It's flexible, that is, you can change your language and tone depending on the situation
you're in or the relationship you share with an individual.

### Non-Verbal Communication
- Non-verbal communication involves transmitting meaning without words. It refers to any
nonverbal way of conveying messages, like in the game "Dumb charades." Non-verbal
communication is generally unintentional, unlike verbal. We tend to communicate silently
and unknowingly signal messages by what we do, not just what we say. Gestures, facial
expressions, posture, and dress are non-verbal communication.

- Non-verbal communication can carry a greater impact than verbal communication because,
sometimes, "how you say something" is more important than "what you say." Although non-
verbal communication affects personal and business relationships, it is particularly vital in
the workplace.
